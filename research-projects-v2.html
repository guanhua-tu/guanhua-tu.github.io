Research Projects @ SNMS

Our research is in computer systems, with a focus on the dependability, security, and privacy of large-scale networked and mobile systems, particularly mobile networks (e.g., 5G/NextG), satellite networks, and wireless IoT, while increasingly exploring the intersection of AI and computer systems. We pursue three research thrusts: the first two secure critical and essential mobile services, and the third develops new methodologies and tools to expose flaws in 3GPP technical specifications and in the non-standardized policies, operations, and requirements that underpin the mobile ecosystem.

Thrust 1: Advancing the Security and Resilience of Next-Generation Emergency Services

  • Security of cellular emergency (9-1-1) services: vulnerabilities rooted in 3GPP/GSMA designs and standards-compliant remedies (MobiCom'22; IEEE/ACM ToN'24).
  • Resilience of cellular emergency services: M911-Verifier, the first model-checking tool for uncovering design flaws that hinder access to cellular emergency services (MobiCom'24).
  • Recognition and impact: ACM MobiCom'22 Best Community Paper Award Runner-Up; ACM GetMobile Research Highlights 2023; ACM SIGMOBILE Research Highlights 2024; GSMA revised its VoLTE Security Analysis and Recommendations; invited presentations to the FCC and 3GPP SA WG3; invited participation in FCC CSRIC WG2 meetings.
  • Supported by a $1.2M NSF SaTC Medium award (Lead PI).

Thrust 2: Taming the Insecurity of Essential Services in Mobile Networked Systems

  • Securing lost/stolen phone reporting services (MobiSys'26): ACM MobiSys'26 Best Paper Award; ACM GetMobile Research Highlights 2026.
  • Securing IMS-based call and text services (CCS'15; CCS'16; MobiCom'24; IEEE ToN'26): ACM GetMobile Research Highlights 2025.
  • Securing cellular IoT services (MobiCom'21; IEEE/ACM ToN'24; IEEE TMC'21).
  • IoT/smart home security: SecWIR, turning commodity Wi-Fi routers into intelligent IoT security gateways (MobiSys'20).
  • Mobile data charging security.

Thrust 3: Developing New Methodologies for Analyzing Technical Specifications and Non-Standardized Policies, Operations, and Requirements

  • Formal model checking for mobile networks: CNetVerifier, the first cellular-specific formal method for protocol interactions (SIGCOMM'14; IEEE/ACM ToN'16), and M911-Verifier (MobiCom'24).
  • Automating specification analysis with large language models: CellSecInspector, an end-to-end LLM-based framework for security analysis of 3GPP specifications (MobiCom'26).

Future Research Directions

  • Securing the space-augmented mobile ecosystem, where satellite connectivity is integrated into cellular infrastructure (e.g., T-Mobile over Starlink).
  • Provable completeness through AI-assisted formal verification.
